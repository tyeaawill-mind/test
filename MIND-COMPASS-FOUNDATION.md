# Mind Compass — Embryonic Foundation v1.0

## 1. Purpose

Mind Compass is a distinct experience within mindRID. It helps a person explore the relationship between what they express, the realities and vulnerabilities they may be responding to, the defensive actions their reactions may perform, and the purposes they consciously choose to pursue.

For users who choose a faith-oriented path, Mind Compass may help them reflect on whether their attention, habits and actions align with seeking Allah's satisfaction. It must never claim to know Allah's judgment, measure a person's piety, or coerce religious participation.

## 2. Relationship to mindRID

- One mindRID platform and account context, subject to explicit identity/privacy controls.
- Two distinct experiences: Social World (Whispers, replies, people, Circles) and Mind Compass (orientation, journeys, exploration, revisiting).
- Existing Whispers remain the canonical expressions. A meteor is a visual representation of a Whisper's journey, not a replacement content object.
- Mind Compass development runs in parallel as preparation; V19 trust work remains first where dependencies require it. V20 is the target for an initial interactive pilot; V21 may expand approved creation tools and shared experiences.
- The confirmed V18.5 production baseline must not be replaced by speculative Compass work.

## 3. Signature interaction: the Whisper meteor

A user chooses to take a Whisper into Compass. It appears as a meteor-like journey with a visible trail. The journey may connect to related Whispers, questions, resources or contrasting perspectives. A user may add a waypoint, branch the journey, save it, revisit it, or record that their interpretation changed.

Progress is not popularity, session length or reaction count. Meaningful progress is user-defined: a connection discovered, a question clarified, a perspective considered, an intention revisited, or a change the user chooses to record.

## 4. Compass orientation

The visual references suggest a cosmic, luminous, dark-cinematic map with four directions:

- Present — what the user chooses to attend to now.
- Future — possibilities, aspirations and perspectives ahead.
- Background — roots, context, prior experiences and grounding.
- Ultimate — enduring purpose and the user's chosen higher-order commitments.

These are provisional labels, user-configurable and not fixed psychological classifications. The visual world should begin as an accessible 2D experience. Motion and effects must be optional, respect reduced-motion preferences, and never obstruct a conventional list-based alternative.

## 5. Approved emotional-defence concept

Human expression may be understood not only by what it communicates, but also by what it may protect. A possible interpretation can explore:

1. Reality or situation encountered.
2. Possible perceived threat.
3. Possible vulnerability.
4. Expressed reaction or thought as a possible defensive action.
5. Intended protection.
6. Possible costs, limits or consequences.
7. Alternative interpretations and responses.

Example: a smile may be a way to reduce conflict with someone perceived as capable of causing harm. This is one possible function, not a universal explanation for smiling. The other person may be genuinely dangerous, the threat may be misperceived, or the smile may have another meaning. Context and the subject's own account matter.

AI must present these as revisable hypotheses, distinguish direct user statements from inference, offer alternative explanations, and permit correction, rejection or deletion. Never diagnose a person, assert access to an unconscious truth, or publicly label another user as defensive.

## 6. Habit awareness and coaching

The product may support users in learning to notice, understand, discern, choose, practise and reorient:

- Notice: what repeatedly attracts attention?
- Understand: what need, concern, reward or vulnerability might be involved?
- Discern: does this serve the user's stated values and aims?
- Choose: what response does the user want to make?
- Practise: what small action could they repeat in ordinary life?
- Reorient: does the user wish to revise their direction?

Dopamine is not directly measurable from clicks, dwell time or return frequency. Those signals cannot establish a user's dopamine state. Start with voluntary self-reflection; later analytics require explicit consent, clear purpose, controls, retention limits and deletion. Use data to support user agency, not covertly exploit reward loops.

## 7. Optional faith-oriented orientation

For users who opt in, prompts may help them reflect on intention, responsibility, restraint, gratitude, service, justice and their understanding of seeking Allah's satisfaction. Keep religious framing transparent and user-controlled. Do not infer religiosity, rank users spiritually, issue divine judgments, shame lapses, or imply that all worldly responsibilities are opposed to faith. The user remains the moral and spiritual decision-maker.

## 8. Non-negotiable product principles

- No drift from the approved purpose without a structured discussion and founder approval.
- Privacy by default for personal maps, attention history and emotional explorations.
- Explicit, granular consent for sharing and sensitive AI processing.
- No covert behavioural manipulation or engagement-at-any-cost optimisation.
- No psychological diagnosis or false certainty about motives.
- Explain why a connection or suggestion appears; allow disable, correction, reset and deletion.
- Do not treat dwell time as proof of interest or infer sensitive traits as fact.
- No autoplay, guilt-inducing drift alerts or compulsive reward mechanics.
- Accessibility: reduced motion, legible contrast, keyboard/screen-reader support and a list alternative.
- Preserve original Whispers and their authorship/context when AI creates summaries or interpretations.

## 9. Product success

Early success is whether users can understand and control the experience, create a journey from a Whisper, discover a useful connection, reject a poor interpretation, and return by choice. Longer-term success may include user-reported clarity, intentional action and alignment with self-chosen purposes. Do not equate success with time-on-platform, number of emotional inferences, spiritual scores or behavioural compliance.
